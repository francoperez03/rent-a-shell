# Rent a Shell

## Register

brand (landing, shell, agents, 404) · product (checkout, session console)

## Users

1. Small hardware teams (2 to 5 people, often bootstrapped) building a physical product with AI help. Context: they have code and no equipment; they want to test today, not in two weeks.
2. Their AI agents (coding agents with MCP or skills support) running tests autonomously via `llms.txt`, x402 and MCP.
3. Forgers: makers and labs that publish idle hardware and get paid per 5-minute block (today one shell, built by us).

## Purpose

Turn "we can't test on real hardware" into a five-cent, five-minute action. Every screen should make the measurement visible: real angles, camera, cost (current in mA once the sensor is on the shell). Trust comes from showing the data, never from claiming it.

## Brand personality

Precise · Warm · Matter-of-fact

A 1975 instrument datasheet written by someone who likes you.

## Anti-references

- Luxury/editorial (serif italics, navy, "premium" whitespace): says agency, not lab.
- Hacker console (black + neon green, mono everywhere): costume, not credibility.
- Crypto landing (gradients, glass, token-ish glow): payment is a detail here.
- Generic SaaS (Inter + purple + three cards with icons).

## Strategic design principles

1. Measurement is the hero: numbers, units and ticks are first-class UI.
2. One accent, used as a signal (status, CTA, live), never as decoration.
3. Paper for reading; dark only where live video and control live (the console).
4. Dense where the user works (console), airy where they decide (landing, checkout).
5. Hardware is real: show the camera, the angles and the cost, never a 3D blob.

## Accessibility

AA minimum everywhere. Keyboard control of the console stays. Reduced motion removes all entrance motion.
